# L18: Probability

**1. Marginalization and Tactical Base Defense**
An automated Counter-Rocket, Artillery, and Mortar (C-RAM) fire-control system maintains a joint probability distribution over incoming projectile trajectories ($T \in \{\text{Inbound}, \text{Outbound}\}$) and adversary electronic spoofing attacks ($S \in \{\text{Spoofed}, \text{Clean}\}$):
*   $P(\text{Inbound}, \text{Spoofed}) = 0.02$
*   $P(\text{Inbound}, \text{Clean}) = 0.08$
*   $P(\text{Outbound}, \text{Spoofed}) = 0.03$
*   $P(\text{Outbound}, \text{Clean}) = 0.87$

Calculate the marginal probability that a projectile is Inbound ($P(\text{Inbound})$) and the marginal probability that the system is experiencing a Spoofing attack ($P(\text{Spoofed})$).

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Calculate the marginal probability $P(\text{Inbound})$ by summing the joint probabilities over all states of variable $S$.
$$P(\text{Inbound}) = P(\text{Inbound}, \text{Spoofed}) + P(\text{Inbound}, \text{Clean})$$
$$P(\text{Inbound}) = 0.02 + 0.08 = 0.10$$

**Step 2:** Calculate the marginal probability $P(\text{Spoofed})$ by summing the joint probabilities over all states of variable $T$.
$$P(\text{Spoofed}) = P(\text{Inbound}, \text{Spoofed}) + P(\text{Outbound}, \text{Spoofed})$$
$$P(\text{Spoofed}) = 0.02 + 0.03 = 0.05$$

**Answer:** $P(\text{Inbound}) = 0.10$ (10% chance of an inbound projectile) and $P(\text{Spoofed}) = 0.05$ (5% chance of an active spoofing attack).

**2. Conditional Probability in Anti-Submarine Warfare (ASW)**
An autonomous Anti-Submarine Warfare (ASW) sonar array detects an acoustic contact. Using the joint probability distribution from Question 1:
*   $P(\text{Inbound}, \text{Spoofed}) = 0.02$
*   $P(\text{Inbound}, \text{Clean}) = 0.08$
*   $P(\text{Outbound}, \text{Spoofed}) = 0.03$
*   $P(\text{Outbound}, \text{Clean}) = 0.87$

If electronic sensors detect that incoming radar signals are actively being spoofed ($S = \text{Spoofed}$), compute the updated conditional probability that the projectile is actually Inbound ($P(\text{Inbound} \mid \text{Spoofed})$).

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** State the conditional probability formula and substitute the known joint probability and marginal evidence.
$$P(\text{Inbound} \mid \text{Spoofed}) = \frac{P(\text{Inbound}, \text{Spoofed})}{P(\text{Spoofed})}$$
From Step 2 of Question 1, $P(\text{Spoofed}) = 0.05$.

**Step 2:** Compute the quotient.
$$P(\text{Inbound} \mid \text{Spoofed}) = \frac{0.02}{0.05} = 0.40$$

**Answer:** $P(\text{Inbound} \mid \text{Spoofed}) = 0.40$ (or 40%). Given that a spoofing attack is occurring, the probability that the track represents a true inbound projectile is 40%.

**3. Evaluating Statistical Independence in Communications Networks**
A military command post utilizes two redundant encrypted data links: Link Alpha ($A$) and Link Bravo ($B$). Let $A=1$ denote that Link Alpha drops packets, and $B=1$ denote that Link Bravo drops packets. The communications diagnostic table shows:
*   $P(A=1) = 0.10$
*   $P(B=1) = 0.20$
*   $P(A=1, B=1) = 0.02$

Mathematically evaluate whether the packet drop events on Link Alpha and Link Bravo are statistically independent.

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** State the mathematical condition required for statistical independence between two events.
$$P(A=1, B=1) = P(A=1) \times P(B=1)$$

**Step 2:** Compute the product of the marginal probabilities and compare it to the observed joint probability.
$$P(A=1) \times P(B=1) = 0.10 \times 0.20 = 0.02$$
The computed product ($0.02$) exactly equals the recorded joint probability $P(A=1, B=1) = 0.02$.

**Answer:** Yes, Link Alpha and Link Bravo are statistically independent because $P(A=1, B=1) = P(A=1)P(B=1) = 0.02$.

</details>
