# L34: Prompt Engineering

**1. Analyzing Prompt Anatomy**

You need an LLM to review a dense, 20-page legal contract regarding drone procurement and explain it to a new pilot. You draft the following prompt:

"Summarize this legal document for a drone pilot. Make sure it isn't too long or confusing."

Identify which of the four core prompt features (Task, Format, Voice, Context) are missing or poorly defined, and rewrite the prompt using the framework to improve the output.

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

Critique of Original Prompt:

Task: Present ("Summarize"), but could be more specific (e.g., "Extract key obligations").

Format: Missing/Vague ("isn't too long"). Needs specific constraints (e.g., "3 bullet points").

Voice: Missing. Needs a specific tone (e.g., "Jargon-free, conversational").

Context: Present ("for a drone pilot"), but contains a negative instruction ("isn't too confusing").

Improved Engineered Prompt:

[TASK] Extract the primary legal obligations and flight restrictions from this contract.

[FORMAT] Provide a 5-point bulleted list, maximum 2 sentences per bullet.

[VOICE] Use a jargon-free, conversational tone.

[CONTEXT] The audience is a new drone pilot with no legal background.

**2. Positive vs. Negative Instructions**

You are trying to get an AI to write a press release about a new radar system, but it keeps using overly aggressive military terminology. You write the prompt: "Write a press release. Do not use scary military words like 'kill', 'destroy', or 'lethal'."

Why will this prompt likely fail to achieve your desired result, and how should you rephrase it?

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>


**Step 1:** Analyze the LLM's language processing. AI models associate strongly with the words you provide in the prompt. By explicitly listing negative words ("kill," "destroy," "lethal"), you are actually injecting those semantic concepts into the model's immediate context window.

**Step 2:** Apply the rule of positive instructions. It is much more effective to tell the AI what you do want it to do, rather than what it should not do.

**Answer:** The negative instructions inadvertently focus the model on aggressive terminology. You should rephrase the prompt using positive commands, such as: "Write a press release. Use a diplomatic, reassuring, and purely defensive tone."


</details>

**3. Meta-Prompting for Accuracy**

You give an LLM a complex logistics problem: "Base A has 50 gallons of fuel. Base B needs 20 gallons. A transport truck burns 1 gallon per mile. The distance is 15 miles. Can the truck deliver the fuel and return to Base A?"

The LLM immediately outputs "Yes, the truck can make the delivery," which is mathematically incorrect. How can you alter your prompt to fix this hallucination without changing the math problem itself?

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Recognize the limitation of standard LLM generation. When forced to provide an immediate answer in the very next token, the model doesn't have "time" to compute complex logic.

**Step 2:** Apply a Meta-Prompt to force step-by-step logic.

**Answer:**  You must append a phrase like "Take a deep breath and work on this problem step-by-step." This forces the Transformer to generate intermediate text explaining the math (e.g., calculating the fuel burn for the outbound trip, the drop-off, and the return trip). These new intermediate tokens are then fed back into the self-attention mechanism, allowing the model to arrive at the correct final conclusion.

</details>
