**1. Confusion Matrix Evaluation**

An autonomous targeting CNN is evaluated on $100$ test images from a recent flight. It correctly identifies $40$ Hostile SAM launchers (True Positives) and correctly ignores $45$ Civilian Trucks (True Negatives). However, it mistakenly flags $5$ Civilian Trucks as SAMs (False Positives), and misses $10$ Hostile SAMs (False Negatives). Calculate the Precision and Recall of the model.

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Calculate Precision (The ratio of correct positive predictions out of all positive predictions).

* $\text{Precision} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}$

* $\text{Precision} = \frac{40}{40 + 5} = \frac{40}{45} \approx 0.888$


**Step 2:**  Calculate Recall (The ratio of correctly identified threats out of all actual threats in the environment).

* $\text{Recall} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}$

* $\text{Recall} = \frac{40}{40 + 10} = \frac{40}{50} = 0.800$

**Answer:** The model has a Precision of 88.8% and a Recall of 80.0%.

</details>

**2. Commander's Intent (Risk Ledger)**

Building on the statistics from Problem 1, your Sector Commander issues a new, strict directive: "We are operating in a highly populated civilian zone. Minimizing collateral damage is our absolute highest priority. I am willing to let some adversary vehicles escape into the mountains to ensure absolutely no civilians are struck."

Based on this directive, should you optimize the CNN's threshold to prioritize Precision or Recall?

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>


**Step 1:** Analyze the commander's risk tolerance. The commander is explicitly forbidding False Positives (False Alarms / Collateral Damage), while accepting the risk of False Negatives (Missed Threats).

**Step 2:** Match the metric to the risk. Precision heavily penalizes False Positives. Recall heavily penalizes False Negatives.

**Answer:** You must optimize the model for Precision. By demanding high Precision, the model will only output a positive targeting solution when it is mathematically certain, drastically reducing the False Positives and satisfying the commander's intent to protect civilians.

**3. Edge Inference Latency**

You are deploying an automated target recognition system to a lightweight edge device (a drone's onboard computer). Model A (ResNet-50) has an inference latency of $250\text{ ms}$ per frame. Model B (MobileNet) has an inference latency of $20\text{ ms}$ per frame.

Calculate the Frames Per Second (FPS) for both models. If an adversary vehicle dashes between two buildings and is only visible to the camera for $0.6$ seconds, how many frames of the target vehicle will each model successfully process?

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>


Step 1: Calculate FPS for both models ($1000\text{ ms}$ divided by latency).

Model A: $\frac{1000}{250} = 4\text{ FPS}$

Model B: $\frac{1000}{20} = 50\text{ FPS}$

Step 2: Calculate processed frames during the $0.6$ second window.

Model A: $4\text{ FPS} \times 0.6\text{ s} = 2.4$ frames.

Model B: $50\text{ FPS} \times 0.6\text{ s} = 30$ frames.

Answer: Model A will only capture 2 frames of the target, whereas Model B will capture 30 frames. This highlights the critical operational need for lightweight architectures when dealing with high-speed tactical edge deployments.

</details>

**4. Fine-Tuning Spatial Hierarchies**

You download a massive pre-trained CNN that was originally trained on millions of ground-level photos. You are adapting it for an MQ-9 Reaper drone looking straight down at the earth from 20,000 feet. You decide to mathematically freeze the first 15 layers of the network, but unfreeze the final 5 layers before retraining.

Conceptually, what spatial features are the frozen layers detecting, and why did you unfreeze the top layers?


<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>


**Step 1:** Identify the role of the early/base layers.

* Early layers learn universal, low-level spatial geometry like straight lines, sharp edges, and simple curves. A vertical line looks the same whether the camera is on the ground or in space. Freezing these layers saves massive amounts of computing power and prevents destroying foundational knowledge.

**Step 2:** Identify the role of the late/top layers.

* Late layers combine those edges into complex, high-level tactical objects. Because a side-profile view of a supply truck (ground-level photo) looks completely different than a top-down view of a supply truck (satellite photo), the pre-trained knowledge is useless here.

**Answer:** The frozen layers are retained to detect foundational lines and edges. The top layers were unfrozen so the model could learn the new, domain-specific spatial geometry associated with overhead, top-down reconnaissance.

</details>
