**Problem 1: Calculating the Full Q-Learning Update**

An autonomous loyal wingman UAV (State $s$) executes a "Flank" maneuver (Action $a$). The agent's current belief for this action is $Q(s, a) = 5.0$. Upon execution, the UAV receives an immediate reward of $r = 2.0$ and transitions to state $s'$, where its onboard sensors calculate that the best next action has an estimated value of $\max_{a'} Q(s', a') = 8.0$. Given a discount factor of $\gamma = 0.5$ and a learning rate of $\alpha = 0.5$, calculate the Target, the Temporal Difference (TD) error ($\delta$), and the updated Q-value $Q_{\text{new}}(s, a)$.

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Calculate the Target combining the immediate reward and the discounted estimated value of the best next action:

$$\text{Target} = r + \gamma \max_{a'} Q(s', a') = 2.0 + 0.5(8.0) = 2.0 + 4.0 = 6.0\text{[cite: 2]}$$

**Step 2:** Calculate the Temporal Difference (TD) error ($\delta$) measuring the discrepancy between the target and the current estimate:

$$\delta = \text{Target} - Q(s, a) = 6.0 - 5.0 = 1.0\text{[cite: 2]}$$

**Step 3:** Compute the adjustment and update the Q-value using the learning rate ($\alpha$):

$$Q_{\text{new}}(s, a) = Q(s, a) + \alpha(\delta) = 5.0 + 0.5(1.0) = 5.5\text{[cite: 2]}$$

**Answer:**

The Target is 6.0, the TD error ($\delta$) is 1.0, and the updated Q-value is 5.5.

</details>

**Interpreting the TD Error and Learning Rate Dynamics**

A cyber-defense agent monitors tactical network traffic and computes a Temporal Difference (TD) error of $\delta = -15$ after rerouting compromised data packets. Does this negative TD error indicate that the outcome was better or worse than the agent's prior belief? If the agent operates with a conservative learning rate of $\alpha = 0.01$, explain how this setting impacts the magnitude of the subsequent Q-value update.


<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Evaluate the sign of the Temporal Difference error ($\delta = \text{Target} - Q(s, a)$). A negative value ($\delta < 0$) indicates that the observed Target fell below the prior prediction $Q(s, a)$, meaning the outcome was worse than expected.

**Step 2:** Assess the impact of the learning rate ($\alpha$) on the adjustment equation ($\alpha \times \delta$). A small learning rate ($\alpha = 0.01$) ensures the agent adjusts its beliefs cautiously, meaning each individual experience has minimal influence and this single negative experience will only slightly reduce the existing Q-value.

**Answer:**

The negative TD error indicates the outcome was worse than expected. Because $\alpha = 0.01$ is very small, the agent modifies its estimate cautiously, resulting in a minor downward adjustment to the current Q-value rather than an erratic drop.

**The Exploration Probability ($\epsilon$) in Electronic Warfare**

An Electronic Warfare (EW) system uses Q-learning to adaptively select jamming frequencies against adversary radar signals. To maximize operational performance immediately upon deployment, an operator recommends setting the exploration probability to $\epsilon = 0$. Explain what behavior $\epsilon = 0$ enforces, and describe the tactical operational risk associated with this greedy strategy.

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Define the behavioral meaning of setting the exploration probability to $\epsilon = 0$. With $\epsilon = 0$, the agent operates with pure exploitation ($1 - \epsilon = 1.0$), always selecting the action with the highest current Q-value and never sampling alternative actions.

**Step 2:** Identify the strategic risks of zero exploration in an unfamiliar or contested environment. Early Q-value estimates are based on limited initial data and are often inaccurate. Without exploration, the system cannot gather new samples to correct erroneous estimates.

**Answer:**

Setting $\epsilon = 0$ forces the EW system to purely exploit its existing Q-table without ever exploring alternative frequencies. The tactical risk is that the system can become trapped using suboptimal jamming bands based on early, flawed estimates, failing to discover more effective jamming frequencies.

**Model-Based Planning vs. Model-Free Experience**

An autonomous UAV navigates contested airspace where the adversary's radar transition dynamics and electronic countermeasures are unknown in advance. Contrast how a model-based algorithm and a model-free Q-learning algorithm approach this scenario. Additionally, list and define each element of the experience tuple that the model-free UAV must record to learn.


<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>


**Step 1:** Compare model-based planning and model-free learning. Model-based algorithms (like Value Iteration) require full advance knowledge of transition probabilities ($P(s' \mid s, a)$) and reward functions ($R$) to calculate expected futures offline. Model-free Q-learning operates without transition probabilities, updating values directly from interaction and observed outcomes.

**Step 2:** Identify and define the four components of the experience packet tuple $(s, a, r, s')$:

* $s$: The current state of the agent before taking an action.


* $a$: The chosen action executed in the environment.


* $r$: The scalar numerical reward observed following the action.


* $s'$: The resulting next state reached after action execution.



**Answer:**

Model-based algorithms require complete prior models ($P(s' \mid s, a)$ and $R$) to compute expectations, whereas model-free Q-learning learns directly from environmental interactions without knowing transition dynamics in advance. The experience packet is $(s, a, r, s')$, where $s$ is the initial state, $a$ is the chosen action, $r$ is the observed reward, and $s'$ is the resulting next state.

</details>
