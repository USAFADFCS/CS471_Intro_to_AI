**1. Embeddings & Cosine Similarity**
Imagine you map three phrases into a 2D vector space. Vector A ("F-35 Engine") is [0.8, 0.9]. Vector B ("Turbine Repair") is [0.7, 0.8]. Vector C ("Canteen Menu") is [0.1, 0.1]. If a user queries "Jet Propulsion" mapped at [0.8, 0.85], how does Cosine Similarity ensure the RAG system ignores Vector C?

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Understand semantic vector space. Vectors pointing in the same direction share similar meanings, while vectors pointing in vastly different directions represent unrelated concepts.

**Step 2:** Apply Cosine Similarity logic. Cosine Similarity calculates the angle between vectors. A value near $1.0$ means they point in the exact same direction (highly similar). A value near $0.0$ means they are perpendicular (unrelated).

**Answer:** The user's query vector [0.8, 0.85] points in almost the exact same direction as Vector A and B, yielding a cosine similarity near $1.0$. Vector C [0.1, 0.1] points in a drastically different direction, yielding a much lower similarity score, mathematically causing the database to filter it out as irrelevant context.

**2. Chunking Strategies**

You have a 10,000-token intelligence report. You decide to chunk the document into sizes of 500 tokens, with a 100-token overlap between each chunk to preserve sentence context. Mathematically, how many new tokens does the system advance for each subsequent chunk?

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Identify the chunk size ($C_s = 500$) and the overlap ($C_o = 100$).

**Step 2:** Recognize that the overlap represents the tokens repeated from the previous chunk.

**Step 3:** Subtract the overlap from the total chunk size to find the stride (the number of new tokens processed per step): $500 - 100 = 400$.

**Answer:** The system advances by 400 new tokens per chunk. Chunk 1 covers tokens 0–500. Chunk 2 covers tokens 400–900. Chunk 3 covers tokens 800–1300, and so on.

**3. Handling Ambiguity (Prompting)**

Your RAG system retrieves two paragraphs regarding troop movements. Chunk 1 says "Target A is lightly defended." Chunk 2 says "Target A has received heavy mechanized reinforcements." If your prompt simply says "Summarize the defense of Target A," what is the operational risk, and how should you alter the prompt to fix it?

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Identify the risk of standard LLM behavior. When faced with conflicting information and a generic prompt, LLMs often attempt to blend the information or arbitrarily pick the claim that looks grammatically superior, resulting in a dangerous hallucination.

**Step 2:** Apply defensive system prompting to handle ambiguity and demand sourcing.

**Answer:** The operational risk is that the model provides a falsely confident, blended answer (e.g., "Target A has light mechanized defenses").
You must alter the prompt to include explicit instructions for conflict resolution: "You must rely ONLY on the provided context. If the context contains conflicting intelligence, do not attempt to guess or blend the data. Explicitly state that reports conflict, quote both claims separately, and cite the chunk number each claim came from."


</details>
