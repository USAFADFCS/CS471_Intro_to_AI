A Blue Force UAV (MAX) is choosing between two routing paths, Alpha and Beta. A Red Force electronic warfare system (MIN) is waiting to jam the UAV in either sector. Based on the Minimax algorithm, trace the evaluation of this game tree. Which path will the UAV choose, and what is the guaranteed mathematical value of that choice?

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Evaluate the terminal outcomes from the perspective of the MIN nodes (bottom-up).
* **MIN Sector 1:** Must choose between yielding an outcome of 15 or 6. As a minimizing agent, it chooses **6** (Jam A2).
* **MIN Sector 2:** Must choose between yielding an outcome of 9 or 11. It chooses **9** (Jam B1).

**Step 2:** Evaluate the MAX root node based on the backed-up values.
* **MAX Root:** Must choose between Path Alpha (which will yield 6) and Path Beta (which will yield 9). As a maximizing agent, it chooses **9**.

**Answer:**
The UAV will choose **Path Beta**. 
Even though Path Alpha has the highest single tactical outcome physically present on the board (15), the UAV knows a rational adversary will never let it achieve that score. By choosing Path Beta, MAX mathematically guarantees a score of at least 9, representing the optimal decision against a perfect opponent.

A commander needs to deploy a surveillance sensor array. There is no active enemy, but the environment is stochastic due to unpredictable weather patterns. Using the Expectimax algorithm, calculate the {term}`Expected Value (EV)` for both deployment options. Which sensor array should the commander choose?

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Calculate the Expected Value for each CHANCE node by multiplying the outcomes by their probabilities.
* **CHANCE Sensor X:** $E[X] = (100 \times 0.7) + (10 \times 0.3) = 70 + 3 = 73$.
* **CHANCE Sensor Y:** $E[Y] = (80 \times 0.8) + (50 \times 0.2) = 64 + 10 = 74$.

**Step 2:** The MAX node evaluates the backed-up EVs and selects the highest mathematical odds.
* **MAX Root:** Chooses between Array X (EV=73) and Array Y (EV=74).

**Answer:**
The commander should select **Array Y**. 
While Array X has the potential for a massive data yield (100), its vulnerability to storms makes it a riskier long-term play. Expectimax maximizes the average payoff over time, making the more consistent Array Y (EV=74) the mathematically optimal choice in this stochastic environment.

**3. Defining a Policy in an Adversarial Game**

```{mermaid}
graph TD

    MAX_S0 -- "Beta" --> MIN_S2((MIN<br>S2))
```

In a complex multi-turn zero-sum game, an agent cannot rely on a static plan; it must define a **Policy** ($\pi$) that maps *every possible state* to an optimal action. 

Based on the deep adversarial tree above:
1. Trace the Minimax algorithm bottom-up to find the final value at `S0`.
2. Extract the exact optimal Policy ($\pi$) for the MAX agent.
3. Explain why the MAX agent needs to define a rule for `S3` and `S4`, even if its optimal first move leads entirely away from those states.

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1: Bottom-up Minimax Evaluation**
* **Level 3 (MAX Nodes):**
  * S3 chooses max(10, 4) $\rightarrow$ **10** (via Act 1)
  * S4 chooses max(5, 2) $\rightarrow$ **5** (via Act 3)
  * S5 chooses max(8, 7) $\rightarrow$ **8** (via Act 5)
  * S6 chooses max(12, 1) $\rightarrow$ **12** (via Act 7)
* **Level 2 (MIN Nodes):**
  * S1 chooses min(10, 5) $\rightarrow$ **5** (via Jam Y)
  * S2 chooses min(8, 12) $\rightarrow$ **8** (via Jam U)
* **Level 1 (MAX Root):**
  * S0 chooses max(5, 8) $\rightarrow$ **8** (via Beta)

**Step 2: Defining the Policy ($\pi$)**
A policy dictates the action MAX should take from *any* MAX node it finds itself in.
* $\pi(S0)$ = **Beta**
* $\pi(S3)$ = **Act 1**
* $\pi(S4)$ = **Act 3**
* $\pi(S5)$ = **Act 5**
* $\pi(S6)$ = **Act 7**

**Step 3: Justifying the full Policy mapping**
If MAX plays optimally at S0 (choosing Beta), it expects to end up at S5 or S6. Why waste compute defining a policy for S3 and S4? 
Because in the real world, MIN might act sub-optimally, the environment might experience random stochastic noise that blows MAX off course, or MAX's evaluation function might have been slightly flawed. If MAX accidentally finds itself in Sector S1, a static plan would shatter. A policy guarantees MAX mathematically knows how to survive and counter-attack (choosing Act 1 or Act 3) from any state in the game, no matter how it got there.

</details>
