# L1: Intro to AI

**1. Hardware vs. Software Agents**

An intelligence squadron deploys a system that automatically translates intercepted foreign radio broadcasts into English text transcripts in real-time. Is this system functioning as a Hardware Agent or a Software Agent?


<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Analyze the system's interface with the environment. Does it have physical actuators (motors, wheels, rotors) to move and manipulate the physical world?

**Step 2:** Determine the input/output. The system takes in data (audio files) and outputs data (text files).

Answer: This is a Software Agent. It processes information and produces an output purely in the digital realm, lacking the physical actuators required to be a hardware agent (autonomous robot).

</details>

**2. Strengths and Limitations**

A commander wants to replace all human imagery analysts with a new AI system that was trained to identify enemy tanks in satellite photos. The AI can process 10,000 photos an hour. However, the adversary recently began hiding their tanks under a new type of optical-camouflage netting that the AI has never seen before. What AI limitation will the commander likely encounter?


<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Acknowledge the AI's strength: Speed and data throughput (10,000 photos an hour).

**Step 2:** Identify the limitation based on the novel camouflage. AI systems rely on historical training data to make predictions.

Answer: The commander will encounter the AI's brittleness in novel environments. Because the AI lacks human adaptability and "common sense," it cannot infer that a strangely shaped lump of netting might be hiding a vehicle. It will likely fail predictably, classifying the camouflaged tanks as empty terrain because the new netting was not in its training data.
</details>

**3. The Cost of Errors**

Referencing the concept of the "Spam Filter", imagine an autonomous sentry gun is programmed to classify approaching figures.

* A False Positive means the gun classifies a friendly soldier as an enemy.

* A False Negative means the gun classifies an enemy infiltrator as a friendly soldier.

Which error carries the highest ethical and tactical cost, and why does this necessitate human evaluation?


<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Define the consequence of a False Positive. (The gun fires on a friendly soldier, resulting in fratricide).

**Step 2:** Define the consequence of a False Negative. (The gun ignores an enemy, allowing an infiltration).

Answer: While both errors are tactically bad, the False Positive carries the highest ethical cost because it results in lethal fratricide (a violation of the rules of engagement). Because an AI will inevitably make statistical errors, a human operator must evaluate the AI's outputs and remain "in the loop" before any lethal action is taken, ensuring the system's mathematical errors do not result in unlawful loss of life.
</details>
