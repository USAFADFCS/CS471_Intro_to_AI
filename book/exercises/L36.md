# L36: Failure Modes and Ethics

**1. Hallucinations vs. Bias**.

An LLM generates a highly detailed, completely fabricated historical account of a battle that never happened. A different CNN correctly identifies enemy tanks, but only in desert environments, failing completely in forests. Which system is suffering from a hallucination, and which is suffering from algorithmic bias?


<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Define the two failure modes. A hallucination is when a model confidently invents false information because it is simply predicting statistically likely word sequences. Algorithmic bias occurs when a model learns a skewed representation of the world due to imbalanced training data.

**Answer:** The LLM inventing a fake battle is suffering from a Hallucination. The CNN failing in forests is suffering from Algorithmic Bias (specifically, it was biased toward desert environments during training and fails to generalize).

</details>

**2. Explainable AI (SHAP)** 

An autonomous medical triage AI recommends a specific treatment for a wounded soldier. The baseline average risk score is 40. The SHAP values for the soldier's data are: Heart Rate (+15), Blood Pressure (+5), and Temperature (-10). What is the model's final predicted risk score for this soldier?

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Understand the fundamental equation of SHAP values. The final prediction of a model is always equal to the baseline average plus the sum of all individual SHAP values.
$\text{Prediction} = \text{Baseline} + \sum \text{SHAP Values}$

**Step 2:** Calculate the sum.
$40 + 15 + 5 - 10 = 50$

**Answer:** The final predicted risk score is 50. The XAI tool successfully explains that the elevated heart rate was the primary driver of the increased risk.

</details>

**3. Data Poisoning vs. Evasion**

An adversary hacks into your training database and subtly alters the labels on images of civilian trucks. Later, in the field, they apply a complex static-pattern sticker to a combat vehicle that causes your deployed camera's gradient calculations to fail. Which attack occurred during training, and which occurred during deployment?

<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>


**Step 1:** Categorize attacks by phase. Attacks that manipulate the data before the model learns (the inside job) are poisoning. Attacks that manipulate the environment to fool an already trained model (the optical illusion) are evasion.

**Answer:** Hacking the database to alter labels is Data Poisoning (training phase). Applying the sticker to fool the camera is Adversarial Evasion (deployment phase).

</details>
