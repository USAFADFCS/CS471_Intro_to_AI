# L19 Bayes' Rule

**1. Probability Interpretation**
A satellite navigation component has a probability of $0.80$ of remaining operational through the next mission segment. How do you interpret this probability in an operational context?


<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Recognize that probability in this context represents the expected frequency over many trials, not a guaranteed certainty for a single event.

**Step 2:** Formulate the interpretation based on the aggregation of many similar segments.

**Answer:** Across many comparable mission segments, the component would be expected to remain operational about 80% of the time, though the outcome of one segment is still uncertain.

</details>

**2. Calculating Joint Probability**
For a reconnaissance system, the probability of adequate visibility is $P(V) = 0.75$, and the probability of reliable communication given adequate visibility is $P(C \mid V) = 0.80$. Compute the requested joint probability $P(V \cap C)$.


<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Apply the product rule for joint probability: $P(V \cap C) = P(V)P(C \mid V)$.

**Step 2:** Substitute the numerical values: $(0.75)(0.80)$.

**Step 3:** Compute the multiplication: $0.75 \times 0.80 = 0.60$.

**Answer:** $0.60$.


</details>

**3. Applying Bayes' Rule**
Given a system failure prior of $P(F) = 0.10$, a sensor likelihood of $P(A \mid F) = 0.80$, and an overall alert probability of $P(A) = 0.20$, calculate the posterior probability $P(F \mid A)$.


<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Set up Bayes' Rule to solve for the posterior: $P(F \mid A) = \frac{P(A \mid F)P(F)}{P(A)}$.

**Step 2:** Substitute the known probabilities into the numerator and denominator: $\frac{(0.80)(0.10)}{0.20}$.

**Step 3:** Evaluate the numerator ($0.80 \times 0.10 = 0.08$) and divide by the denominator ($0.08 / 0.20 = 0.40$).

**Answer:** $0.40$.

</details>

**4. Base Rate Reasoning**
A detector identifies 95% of actual failures, but failures occur in only 1% of missions. Why might an alert still have a posterior probability far below the detector's detection rate?


<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1:** Identify the relationship between the base rate (the 1% true failure population) and the non-failure population (the remaining 99%).

**Step 2:** Reason about the raw volume of false alerts generated by testing a massive non-failure population compared to the true alerts generated from a tiny failure population.

**Answer:** Failures are rare, so false alerts from the much larger no-failure population may be numerous. The detection rate is $P(A \mid F)$, not the posterior $P(F \mid A)$.

</details>
