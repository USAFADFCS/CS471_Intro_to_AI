1. Identifying Parents and Ancestors

An Electronic Warfare (EW) surveillance network is structured as follows:


$$\text{RadarJamming} \to \text{ReceiverNoise}, \quad \text{TerrainMasking} \to \text{SignalAttenuation}$$

$$\text{ReceiverNoise} \to \text{BitErrorRate}, \quad \text{SignalAttenuation} \to \text{BitErrorRate}$$

$$\text{BitErrorRate} \to \text{PacketLoss}$$

List all immediate parents of $\text{BitErrorRate}$, and determine whether $\text{RadarJamming}$ is an ancestor of $\text{PacketLoss}$.

Step 1: Identify incoming edges to $\text{BitErrorRate}$.
Directed edges point from $\text{ReceiverNoise}$ and $\text{SignalAttenuation}$ into $\text{BitErrorRate}$.
Therefore, $Parents(\text{BitErrorRate}) = \{\text{ReceiverNoise}, \text{SignalAttenuation}\}$.

Step 2: Check for a directed path from $\text{RadarJamming}$ to $\text{PacketLoss}$.
Path: $\text{RadarJamming} \to \text{ReceiverNoise} \to \text{BitErrorRate} \to \text{PacketLoss}$.
Because a continuous downstream path exists, $\text{RadarJamming}$ is an ancestor of $\text{PacketLoss}$.

Answer: The parents of $\text{BitErrorRate}$ are $\text{ReceiverNoise}$ and $\text{SignalAttenuation}$. Yes, $\text{RadarJamming}$ is an ancestor of $\text{PacketLoss}$.

2. Completing Conditional Independence Statements

In an autonomous convoy defense network, an adversary Electronic Attack aircraft ($EA$) affects both the convoy's GPS receiver ($G$) and its Short-Range Radio ($R$): $EA \to G$ and $EA \to R$. Both $G$ and $R$ feed data into the Automated Navigation Computer ($N$): $G \to N$ and $R \to N$.

Complete the conditional independence statement: $G \perp R \mid \rule{2cm}{0.15mm}$, and explain why this independence holds.

Step 1: Identify the common cause structure.
$G$ and $R$ share an upstream common parent, $EA$.

Step 2: Apply the diverging fork property.
Conditioning on the common parent blocks information flow between the two child nodes: $P(G, R \mid EA) = P(G \mid EA)P(R \mid EA)$.

Answer: $G \perp R \mid EA$. Once the operational status of the adversary Electronic Attack aircraft ($EA$) is observed, knowing the degradation level of the GPS provides no additional information about the state of the radio.

3. Factoring the Joint Distribution

Given the tactical network $W \to V, W \to C, V \to M, C \to M$, suppose the local CPT values are:

$P(W = \text{Clear}) = 0.80$

$P(V = \text{Good} \mid W = \text{Clear}) = 0.90$

$P(C = \text{Reliable} \mid W = \text{Clear}) = 0.85$

$P(M = \text{Success} \mid V = \text{Good}, C = \text{Reliable}) = 0.95$

Compute the full joint probability $P(W = \text{Clear}, V = \text{Good}, C = \text{Reliable}, M = \text{Success})$.

Step 1: State the factored joint distribution equation for the DAG:


$$P(W, V, C, M) = P(W) \cdot P(V \mid W) \cdot P(C \mid W) \cdot P(M \mid V, C)$$

Step 2: Substitute the known numerical values:


$$P(\text{Clear}, \text{Good}, \text{Reliable}, \text{Success}) = (0.80) \times (0.90) \times (0.85) \times (0.95)$$

Step 3: Calculate the product:

$$0.80 \times 0.90 = 0.72$$

$$0.72 \times 0.85 = 0.612$$

$$0.612 \times 0.95 = 0.5814$$

Answer: $0.5814$ (or $58.14\%$).
